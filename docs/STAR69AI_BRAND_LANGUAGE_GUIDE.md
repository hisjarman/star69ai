# Star69AI Official Brand Language Guide

**Status:** Official working standard for all new Star69AI content and material revisions  
**Applies to:** Public website, client workspace, Command Center, marketing and transactional email, automated campaigns, blog/Insights, social content, partner communications, support and product copy.

## 1. Brand identity

**Brand:** Star69AI  
**Tagline:** Communications, Evolved.  
**Founder signature:** Solomon Jarman · Founder & Operator  
**Voice:** Confident, premium, futuristic, human, helpful and precise.

The creative inspiration is classic space exploration, starship command environments, discovery and communications. The execution must remain original to Star69AI. Do not imply an affiliation with Star Trek or another franchise, and do not use franchise-specific names, insignia, dialogue, fictional organizations, interfaces or protected product terminology as Star69AI product names.

Star69AI is a managed, done-for-you AI receptionist service—not a DIY software platform. Keep the real business value clear: Star69AI configures, customizes, tests and maintains the receptionist for the client.

## 3. Rules for consistent use

1. Prefer **Crew** over generic “team” in customer-facing brand communications when it reads naturally.
2. Use **Mission Control** consistently for the client dashboard and **Command Center** for the internal admin console. Do not interchange them.
3. **Navigator** means the AI assistant, not the customer's AI receptionist service itself. Keep “AI receptionist” explicit when describing the product being sold.
4. Use **Open Channel** for contact pathways and **Initiate Contact** for demo/inquiry actions. Keep the action destination obvious.
5. Use **Mission Briefing** for onboarding and **Systems Online** only when setup is truly complete. Never imply activation, live-call readiness or successful integration before verification.
6. Use **Mission Status** for progress. “Mission Report” may be used as a branded secondary label for a call summary, but the summary must remain immediately recognizable.
7. Use **Alliance Program** and **Alliance Member** in partner marketing. Keep commission, payout, discount, eligibility and contractual terms in plain, exact language.
8. Use spaceflight vocabulary as a light, coherent layer—not as a replacement for the meaning of a sentence. One well-placed term is better than several forced terms.
9. On first use in a new or important workflow, pair a branded term with a plain-language explanation where needed (for example, “Mission Control — your client dashboard”).
10. Preserve clear, literal language for pricing, billing, contracts, privacy, consent, call recording, security, legal notices, unsubscribe/opt-out, support instructions and error messages.

## 4. Email and campaign standards

- Use the Star69AI voice: concise, warm, confident, credible and specific to the recipient.
- Address recipients as clients, business owners or partners as appropriate; do not call prospects “crew” before they have a relationship with Star69AI.
- Use “Our Crew” or “Client Care Crew” when referring to the people who configure, test, maintain or support a client's service.
- Use subject lines such as “Your Mission Control Update” or “A New Contact for Your Business” only when the content genuinely supports them. Do not force science-fiction language into every subject.
- Keep the managed-service proposition prominent. Never describe Star69AI as a DIY platform or imply the customer must build and maintain their own agent.
- Marketing/prospecting emails must retain the required **ADVERTISEMENT** disclosure, sender identity, business contact details, unsubscribe link and applicable unsubscribe headers. Do not add advertising disclosure to ordinary transactional or support emails unless they contain advertising.
- Preserve the approved marketing signature: **Solomon Jarman · Founder & Operator**, linked to the About page. Do not add duplicate generic brand sign-offs or a second logo/tagline block.
- Never send an email merely because a draft was generated. Existing individual review, explicit manual send action, unsubscribe rules, cooldowns and rate limits remain mandatory.

## 5. Website, product and content applications

- **Public website:** Use “Initiate Contact” for the primary inquiry/demo action where the current design supports it; “Open Channel” for contact pathways; explain the managed service in direct business language.
- **Client portal:** Use “Mission Control” as the workspace identity. “Crew Support” may identify support; “Navigator” is reserved for the AI assistant.
- **Admin:** Use “Command Center” as the internal operations identity. Do not expose internal-only terminology in customer-facing messages.
- **Onboarding:** “Mission Briefing” may label the onboarding experience. “Systems Online” is a completion status, not a marketing promise.
- **Calls and reporting:** “Incoming Transmission” and “Mission Report” may be used as decorative/secondary labels, but retain plain “Call,” “Recording,” “Transcript” and “Summary” in operational controls and disclosures.
- **Blog and social:** The science-fiction atmosphere can add personality, but the article must still solve the reader's real business problem. Keep SEO terms natural and the claims accurate.
- **Partners:** Use “Alliance Program” and “Alliance Member” in promotional contexts. Keep payout and commission calculations unambiguous.

## 6. Sample brand phrases

- “Your Mission. Our Crew. Always Connected.”
- “Your Mission Control is ready.”
- “Open a Channel with the Star69AI Crew.”
- “Your receptionist is configured and ready for its final systems check.” (Only when accurate.)
- “We've received your transmission. Our Crew will follow up shortly.”
- “Communications, Evolved.”

These are examples, not mandatory boilerplate. Choose clarity and relevance over repetition.

## 7. Quality-control checklist

Before approving new or revised copy, confirm:

- Star69AI is presented as a managed service, not DIY software.
- Crew terminology is used naturally and consistently.
- Mission Control, Command Center and Navigator are used for their distinct meanings.
- Branded phrases do not obscure the actual action, capability or status.
- No unverified booking, integration, activation, response-time or business outcome is promised.
- Pricing, billing, privacy, recording, consent, legal and opt-out wording remains literal and precise.
- Marketing email disclosures, founder signature, contact details and unsubscribe protections are preserved.
- No protected Star Trek/franchise-specific names, insignia, dialogue or copied interface elements are used as Star69AI branding.
