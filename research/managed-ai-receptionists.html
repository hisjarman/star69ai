THE MANAGED MODEL

Done-for-you does not mean hands-off decisions.

A responsible agency does the configuration work, but the business owner still approves what the receptionist may say, which calls need a person, what data can be collected and how exceptions are handled.

What a managed engagement should include

  • Discovery of services, hours, caller types and service boundaries.
  • Call-flow design, approved knowledge and escalation paths.
  • Voice, number and integration configuration within agreed scope.
  • Scenario testing and owner review before production use.
  • Documented change requests, monitoring and ongoing maintenance terms.
  • Clear reporting on what happened—and what the system could not do.
QUESTIONS TO ASK ANY PROVIDER

Compare operating details, not slogans.

What exactly is included?

Ask about setup, call minutes, phone numbers, integrations, support, revisions and after-hours behavior.

Who tests the call flow?

Request realistic test scenarios, failure handling and approval before live calls are routed.

What happens on exceptions?

Understand transfer failures, urgent calls, outages, caller complaints and fallback coverage.

Who controls the data?

Review recording, transcription, retention, access, deletion, subprocessors and contractual protections.

How are charges calculated?

Confirm included usage, rounding, overages, telecom fees, taxes, setup charges and annual terms in writing.

Can I leave or change providers?

Ask who owns phone numbers, prompts, call-flow documentation and exportable call records at termination.
