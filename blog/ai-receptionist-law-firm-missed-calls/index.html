SCENE 1 · THREE PROBLEMS BEFORE 9:15

The Case of the Missing Phone Answer

At 9:12 on Thursday morning, Martin Bell had three problems. A commercial lease agreement was waiting for review. His printer had decided that page 47 of a 46-page document was essential. And the office phone was ringing. Martin was a small-business attorney who handled contracts, business disputes, and the occasional client who described a 90-page agreement as “just a quick thing.” His assistant was helping another client. Martin looked at the phone, then at the printer. “One of you is going to have to cooperate,” he muttered. The printer made a noise that sounded suspiciously like a laugh.

SCENE 2 · THE PROSPECTIVE CLIENT

THE PROSPECTIVE CLIENT

A new caller owned a small retail business and wanted an attorney to review a commercial lease before making a decision. She had a deadline coming up and wanted to know whether the firm handled that kind of work. The AI receptionist answered using the firm’s approved intake process. It collected her name, callback number, business name, general matter category, preferred contact time, and names of the other parties for the firm’s conflict-check process. It captured when she needed a response for attorney review. It did not invite her to disclose confidential details or upload sensitive documents before the firm had reviewed the intake and determined whether it could speak with her. It explained that the call did not establish an attorney-client relationship or confirm representation. No legal opinions. No promises. Just a useful intake record.

SCENE 3 · AN EXISTING CLIENT

AN EXISTING CLIENT

A second caller was an existing client checking whether a document had been received. The receptionist followed the firm’s configured process, collected the client’s name and callback number, and captured the nature of the question for the assigned team. It did not reveal case details or discuss confidential information without the firm’s required verification. The message was routed to the appropriate staff queue. Martin finally freed page 47 from the printer and held it up triumphantly. “Exhibit A!” His assistant walked past. “That’s the printer manual.” Martin lowered the paper. “Then I have discovered the real defendant.”

SCENE 4 · THE INTAKE REVIEW

THE INTAKE REVIEW

After his meeting, Martin reviewed the call summaries. The prospective client had provided information for an initial intake and conflict-check review. Her timing concern was clearly noted. The existing client had a message waiting for the appropriate team member. Martin could review the requests, determine whether the firm could consider the prospective matter, and arrange appropriate follow-up. The AI had not reviewed the lease, interpreted a legal deadline, recommended a course of action, or accepted a case. That was Martin’s job. It had simply made sure the calls were answered and the information was captured while the lawyers and staff were occupied.

SCENE 4½ · THE FIRM’S SAFEGUARDS

Intake is not representation

Martin’s office had clear rules for what an initial call could collect and who could see it. Party names were sent for the firm’s conflict-review process, while sensitive documents and detailed facts waited until an authorized team member determined the next appropriate step. The receptionist’s confirmation made no promise that the firm would accept the matter, and the caller was told not to rely on the intake as legal advice or a deadline calculation. Those safeguards were not fine print to Martin; they were part of running a professional office. A neat intake summary helped his team respond, but the legal judgment stayed with the lawyers.

THE LAST WORD

The last word

Martin looked at his calendar, then the printer, then the phone. Silence. “Finally,” he said. “Order in the court.” The printer immediately started printing another document. His assistant checked the screen. “That’s the printer queue.” Martin straightened his tie, picked up his coffee, and declared: “I move that we replace the printer with a competent witness.” From reception, the AI receptionist cheerfully answered another call. At least one member of the office was consistently prepared to take a statement. Case closed. Phone answered. Printer still under investigation.

THE BUSINESS TAKEAWAY

Keep doing the work. Let the phone get answered.

Frequently asked questions

Can an AI receptionist answer law-firm calls?

Yes. It can greet callers, collect basic information, capture general matter categories, and route messages using firm-approved procedures.

Can it collect prospective-client intake?

It can collect approved contact details and general intake information. The firm should control what is collected before conflicts are reviewed.

Can it perform conflict checks?

It can collect party names for the firm’s process. The law firm must perform and approve the actual conflict review.

Can it give legal advice or calculate deadlines?

No. Legal advice, deadline interpretation, confidentiality decisions, and representation decisions require qualified legal professionals.
