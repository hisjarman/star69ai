2. Prepare for your Mission Briefing

Gather these details before configuration. Do not send passwords, payment-card details or unnecessary sensitive records in an ordinary form or support ticket.

  • Business name and correct pronunciation.
  • Locations, time zone, normal and holiday hours.
  • Services offered, exclusions, service area and frequently asked questions.
  • What the receptionist may say about pricing—or when it must refer pricing to a person.
  • What details to collect: name, callback number, requested service, location, urgency and preferred callback time.
  • Who receives leads, who handles urgent calls and what happens if nobody answers.
  • Appointment workflow and the calendar or system that confirms availability, if connected.
  • Required languages, pronunciation examples and recording/transcription preferences.
  • Backup process if the phone service or a transfer destination is unavailable.

Important: A demo, portal account or payment receipt does not by itself mean your production phone number is assigned or live. Star69AI confirms the number, agent, routing and test call before activation.

3. What a good receptionist should do

Understand intent

Recognize whether the caller needs a quote, service, appointment, existing-customer help or a human.

Capture useful details

Collect only the information needed to route and follow up on the inquiry.

Respect boundaries

Use approved facts. Admit uncertainty rather than inventing answers.

Escalate correctly

Follow the agreed urgent-call, transfer and no-answer fallback rules.

A receptionist should not invent a price, promise a technician's arrival, diagnose a problem, guarantee emergency dispatch or claim an appointment is booked unless an authoritative connected system confirms it.

4. Understand Receptionist Intelligence

Receptionist Intelligence reviews completed call information to help identify caller intent, outcomes, missing lead details, recurring questions, friction and possible improvements. It can prepare recommendations for the Star69AI Crew to review and test.

What it can indicate

  • Potential lead and requested service
  • Whether callback details appear complete
  • Questions the agent could not answer
  • Possible missed opportunities or friction

What it cannot prove by itself

  • A confirmed booking or completed job
  • Actual revenue or recovered sales
  • Customer satisfaction or intent with certainty
  • That a suggested prompt change is safe to publish

Human review matters. AI findings are assessments, not guaranteed facts. High-impact or low-confidence findings should be checked against the call. Live receptionist changes are reviewed, scenario-tested and approved before release.

5. Read call records and follow up

  1. Open Call activity. Review the date, caller, duration, status and available summary.
  2. Open Details. Check the transcript or recording if available and authorized.
  3. Verify the lead. Confirm callback number, service, location, urgency and what the caller actually requested.
  4. Assign a person. Decide who will call back and by when. The AI receptionist does not replace your team's follow-up.
  5. Close the loop. Record the real outcome in your business system so future reviews can distinguish inquiries from booked work.

Not every call will have a transcript, recording or complete summary. Availability depends on provider processing and account configuration. Share call details only with people authorized to receive them.

7. Keep the receptionist accurate

Tell Star69AI when your hours, service area, pricing, staff, phone numbers, appointment process, emergency contacts, policies or supported languages change. Use the Receptionist configuration request for planned changes and Crew Support for faults or urgent issues.

  • Request a change with the exact wording or desired outcome.
  • Identify which services, callers or locations are affected.
  • Explain whether the change is urgent and when it should take effect.
  • Review any quote and approve the scope before paid work starts.
  • Test the updated behavior before it is relied upon in production.

8. Privacy, recordings and security

Only collect information needed to handle the business inquiry. Limit access to call recordings, transcripts and summaries. Keep notification recipients current and authorized. Recording and transcription notices, consent, retention and deletion depend on your configuration and applicable law; confirm the required process before launch. Never share passwords or card details in support tickets.
